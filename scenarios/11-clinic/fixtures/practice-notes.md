  sounds.

**Room 2 has the traction bed**, so anything lumbar traction has to be Room 2, and Room 2 is also
the only one with a plinth low enough for the older patients. Room 1 is fine for everything else.
This one constraint causes about half of the rescheduling I do.

## How a course of treatment works

- A GP or a surgeon refers someone. **A referral is only good for 90 days** from the date on it —
  after that the insurer won't pay and we have to send them back for a new one. I have absolutely
  lost money on this more than once.
- **A course is six sessions.** At session six we either discharge or write to the referrer for
  another six. Nobody gets session seven without that letter, and I forget to write it.
- Post-op knees are typically weekly for the first six weeks, then fortnightly.
- Everyone gets a home exercise programme. I write it on a photocopied sheet like it's 1998.

## The thing that actually ruins my week

**No-shows.** A no-show at 08:00 is an hour of my life I don't get back, and it's almost always the
same handful of people. House rule, and I want it applied without me having to remember it:
**two strikes and they lose the 8am slot** — after two no-shows that patient only gets offered
afternoon appointments. I never enforce it because I never notice the second one until it's the
third one.

I also want to know, at a glance, who has no-showed *this month* and what it cost us, because when
I look at it as a number I might finally do something about it.

## Money

- We bill per session, per code. The code list is in the other file.
- **A 40-minute manual therapy session is €38.50** and that's the one we do most.
- The insurers pay in arrears, badly. Anything unbilled after two weeks is money I've lent someone
  for free.
- Some patients are private and pay on the day; those are the ones I forget to record at all.
