# What Marcus and I actually need to keep on top of

(Nadia. Typed on my phone standing outside Elm Court, so forgive the tone.)

## The portfolio

Fourteen flats over three buildings, all in Sheffield. Marcus does the maintenance side, I do the
money side, and we both do the worrying.

- **Ravensworth House** — six flats (1A, 1B, 2A, 2B, 2C, 3A). Victorian conversion, our first
  building and still the nicest. **1B has been empty since April** and every week it's empty costs
  us about two hundred quid. EPC certificate on the building runs to 2031.
- **Elm Court** — five flats (1 to 5). Ex-council block, bought in 2019. **The boiler is shared and
  it is on its last legs** — when it goes, it goes for all five at once, which is the thing that
  wakes me up at 4am.
- **Hawthorn Rise** — three flats (1A, 2B, 3B). Newest, least trouble, except that **3B pays
  weekly, always has** — he gets paid weekly, he's never going to change, and every month it looks
  like he's behind when he isn't quite.

## The rules we work to

- **Rent is due on the 1st.** Everyone. No exceptions, including 3B, whose weekly payments just have
  to add up to the month by the end of it.
- **We chase at 14 days.** A polite message at fourteen days past due, a proper letter at thirty.
- **Nothing formal goes out without me saying so.** Not a notice, not a letter before action, not
  anything with a solicitor's name on it. I have been burned by a letter that went out on a Friday
  to a tenant who'd already paid on the Thursday.
- **Deposits are all in the DPS**, protected within 30 days of the tenancy starting, and the
  protection reference has to be given to the tenant. If we miss that window it is a three-times-the
  -deposit problem, which is not a mistake you get to make twice.
- **Gas safety certificate every 12 months, per flat.** The one at Elm Court 4 is the one I always
  forget because it was done out of cycle after the boiler repair.

## What actually goes wrong

Two things, and they're both the same thing: **I can't see who's behind and by how much** until I
sit down with a spreadsheet on a Sunday night, by which point someone is thirty days over and I'm
having a difficult conversation I could have had at fourteen days for free. And **the repairs come
in by text**, to two different phones, and get forgotten unless one of us writes it on the back of
an envelope.

I'd also love to just *see* each building — which flats are full, which are empty, which are behind
— instead of reading a list of flat numbers and translating them in my head every time.

## What we're leaving behind

The old letting software exports two files and then charges us £40 a month for the privilege: the
tenancy list and the whole rent ledger. Both are attached. The ledger has some rubbish in it from
the migration they did in 2024 — a few rows with dates that aren't dates and at least one flat
reference that has never existed. Whatever we do next has to tell me about those rather than
quietly swallowing them, because last time something quietly swallowed them we were out £700 and
didn't notice for five months.
