# Diabetic Diet

## Foods to Include

### Recommended

- **Non-starchy vegetables:** Leafy greens (spinach, kale, arugula), broccoli, cauliflower, bell peppers, zucchini, asparagus, green beans, mushrooms, tomatoes. Unlimited intake — these have minimal impact on blood glucose and provide fiber, vitamins, and antioxidants.
- **Lean protein:** Skinless poultry, fish (especially fatty fish for omega-3), eggs, tofu, tempeh, legumes. Protein has minimal direct effect on blood glucose and increases satiety. Distribute protein across all meals.
- **Whole grains (portion-controlled):** Quinoa, brown rice, barley, oats, whole wheat bread, whole wheat pasta. Portion size is critical — 1/3 cup cooked grains or 1 slice bread equals one carb serving (15 g carbohydrate). Whole grains have a lower glycemic index than refined grains and provide fiber.
- **Legumes and lentils:** Low glycemic index due to high fiber and protein content. Chickpeas, black beans, kidney beans, lentils. One serving (1/2 cup cooked) has ~20 g carbohydrate but a glycemic load of only 6-8.
- **Healthy fats:** Avocado, nuts (almonds, walnuts, pistachios), seeds (chia, flax, pumpkin), olive oil, fatty fish. Monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fats improve insulin sensitivity and cardiovascular risk. Omega-3 fatty acids (2+ fish servings per week) are especially important given the elevated cardiovascular risk in diabetes.
- **Low-glycemic fruits:** Berries (strawberries, blueberries, raspberries), cherries, apples, pears, citrus, kiwi. Pair with protein or fat to blunt glycemic response. Limit to 2-3 servings per day (1 serving = 1 small fruit or 1/2 cup).
- **Fermented foods:** Unsweetened yogurt (Greek preferred for higher protein), kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut. Emerging evidence links gut microbiome health to insulin sensitivity.

## Foods to Avoid or Limit

### High-Glycemic and Refined Carbohydrates

- Sugar-sweetened beverages: soda, juice, sweetened tea/coffee, energy drinks. These cause the most rapid glucose spikes. A single 12 oz soda contains 39 g sugar — more than a day's worth of carbohydrate in one serving.
- White bread, white rice, white pasta, pastries, muffins, donuts, cereals with added sugar. These have high glycemic indices (70-100) and provide minimal nutritional value.
- Candy, cookies, cake, ice cream, syrup, honey, agave (all concentrated sugars regardless of source).

### Other

- Fried foods: Increase insulin resistance and cardiovascular risk.
- Processed meats: Associated with increased type 2 diabetes risk (50 g/day processed meat increases risk by ~19%).
- Alcohol: Can cause unpredictable hypoglycemia in type 1 diabetes and patients on insulin or sulfonylureas. If consumed, limit to 1 drink/day (women) or 2 drinks/day (men), always with food. Avoid sweet cocktails and dessert wines.

## Meal Structure

- **Consistent carbohydrate timing and amount** is the foundation. The meal plan should distribute carbohydrates across meals to match the action profile of the patient's diabetes medication.
- **Type 1 diabetes (or type 2 on basal-bolus insulin):** Carbohydrate counting with insulin-to-carb ratios. Typically 45-60 g carb per meal for women, 60-75 g for men, adjusted individually. Matching rapid-acting insulin to carbohydrate intake using a ratio (e.g., 1 unit per 10 g carb).
- **Type 2 diabetes (oral medications or GLP-1 RA):** Focus on carbohydrate quality over strict counting. Aim for 30-45 g carb per meal from low-glycemic sources. GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide) significantly reduce appetite and should be accompanied by nutrient-dense food choices to avoid malnutrition during weight loss.
- **Meal spacing:** 3 meals per day at regular intervals (every 4-5 hours). Avoid prolonged fasting which can lead to overeating at the next meal. An evening snack may be needed if dinner is early or if the patient is on evening insulin.
- **Plate method:** Fill 1/2 plate with non-starchy vegetables, 1/4 plate with lean protein (3-4 oz), 1/4 plate with whole grain or starchy food. Add a small serving of fruit and/or dairy if carbohydrate budget allows.

## Nutrient Targets

| Nutrient | Target | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Carbohydrate | 40-50% of total calories | Individualize based on medication, activity, and glycemic response |
| Added sugars | <5% of total calories | WHO recommends <5% for additional benefits |
| Protein | 15-20% of calories (0.8-1.0 g/kg/day for normal renal function) | Increase to 1.0-1.5 g/kg/day with GLP-1 RA therapy to preserve lean mass during weight loss |
| Total fat | 25-35% of calories | Emphasize MUFA and PUFA |
| Fiber | 25-30 g/day (aim for 14 g per 1,000 kcal) | Soluble fiber improves postprandial glucose |
| Sodium | <2,300 mg/day | Tighten to <1,500 mg/day if hypertensive |
| Alcohol | <=1 drink/day (women), <=2 (men) | Count alcohol carbohydrate; risk of hypoglycemia with insulin |

## Relationship to Conditions

- **Type 2 diabetes and obesity:** Weight loss of 5-10% improves glycemic control, and 10-15% can achieve diabetes remission in some patients. GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors facilitate weight loss and provide cardiovascular and renal protection independent of glycemic control.
- **Diabetic kidney disease:** Transition to the renal diet principles as eGFR declines below 45. Reduce protein to 0.6-0.8 g/kg/day. Limit potassium and phosphorus as labs dictate. SGLT2 inhibitors remain indicated down to eGFR 20 mL/min for renal protection.
- **Cardiovascular disease and diabetes:** Diabetes is a cardiovascular risk equivalent. The dietary pattern should simultaneously address glycemic control and cardiovascular risk reduction (see Cardiac Diet). The Look AHEAD trial showed that intensive lifestyle intervention reduced cardiovascular risk factors even though the primary endpoint was not met.
- **Gestational diabetes:** Carbohydrate is distributed across 3 meals and 2-3 snacks, with a bedtime snack to prevent overnight ketosis and fasting hyperglycemia. Target fasting glucose <95 mg/dL, 1-hour postprandial <140 mg/dL. Insulin therapy if dietary management alone does not achieve targets within 1-2 weeks.
- **Hypoglycemia management:** Treat with 15-20 g fast-acting glucose (4 oz juice, 3-4 glucose tablets, 1 tbsp honey). Recheck glucose in 15 minutes. Repeat if less than 70 mg/dL. Follow with a protein-containing snack once stable. Glucagon emergency kit for severe hypoglycemia (glucose <54 mg/dL with altered consciousness).
