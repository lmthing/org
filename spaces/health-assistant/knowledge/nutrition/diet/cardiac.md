# Cardiac Diet

## Foods to Include

### Recommended

- **Fatty fish:** Salmon, mackerel, sardines, trout, herring. Aim for 2+ servings per week (EPA + DHA intake of at least 500 mg/day). For patients with established coronary artery disease, 1 g/day of EPA+DHA via supplements may be considered.
- **Whole grains:** Oats, barley, brown rice, quinoa, whole wheat bread (100% whole wheat). Soluble fiber from oats and barley (beta-glucan) reduces LDL cholesterol by 5-10% at intakes of 3 g/day.
- **Vegetables and fruits:** At least 5 servings per day (aim for variety of colors). Rich in potassium (helps blood pressure), antioxidants, and fiber. Dark leafy greens, berries, citrus, tomatoes, cruciferous vegetables.
- **Legumes:** Beans, lentils, chickpeas. Replace animal protein 2-3 times per week. Associated with 10% reduction in cardiovascular events per meta-analysis.
- **Nuts and seeds:** Walnuts, almonds, flaxseeds, chia seeds. 1-1.5 oz/day (about a handful). Associated with reduced cardiovascular mortality. High in unsaturated fats, fiber, and plant sterols.
- **Healthy oils:** Extra virgin olive oil (primary cooking fat), avocado oil, canola oil. Replace butter and tropical oils.
- **Plant sterols/stanols:** 2 g/day from fortified foods (margarines, orange juice) can reduce LDL by 8-10%.

## Foods to Avoid or Limit

### Saturated and Trans Fats

- Red meat (beef, lamb, pork): limit to lean cuts, 6 oz or less per week. Trim all visible fat.
- Full-fat dairy: butter, whole milk, cream, regular cheese. Replace with low-fat or plant-based alternatives.
- Tropical oils: coconut oil, palm oil, palm kernel oil. Despite marketing, these raise LDL cholesterol.
- Processed meats: bacon, sausage, hot dogs, salami, deli meats. Strong independent association with cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality.
- Trans fats: partially hydrogenated oils (largely removed from US food supply but still present in some baked goods and fried foods). No safe level of intake. Check labels for "partially hydrogenated."

### Sodium (Heart Failure Specific)

- Limit to less than 1,500-2,000 mg/day for heart failure (HFrEF and HFpEF).
- Avoid: canned soups, frozen meals, fast food, processed cheeses, salted snacks, soy sauce, pickled foods, deli meats.
- Cooking tip: Use herbs, spices, citrus, vinegar, and salt-free seasoning blends instead of salt at the table and in cooking.

### Added Sugars

- Limit to less than 25 g/day (women) or 36 g/day (men) per AHA guidelines.
- Sugar-sweetened beverages are the single largest source of added sugars. Replace with water, unsweetened tea, or coffee.
- Limit sweets, pastries, and desserts to occasional consumption.

## Meal Structure

- **3 meals + 1 optional snack per day.** Consistent meal timing helps regulate blood pressure and lipid levels.
- **Plate method:** Fill half the plate with vegetables, one quarter with lean protein (fish, poultry, legumes), one quarter with whole grains or starchy vegetables.
- **Breakfast:** Emphasize whole grains (oatmeal with berries and nuts), whole wheat toast with avocado, or Greek yogurt with fruit and seeds.
- **Lunch and dinner:** Lead with a large salad or vegetable-based soup. Include a fish-based meal at least twice per week.
- **Snacks:** Fresh fruit, unsalted nuts, vegetables with hummus, or a small portion of dark chocolate (>70% cocoa).

## Nutrient Targets

| Nutrient | Target | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Total fat | 25-35% of calories | Emphasize unsaturated sources |
| Saturated fat | <7% of calories (<5% for high LDL) | Replace with MUFA/PUFA, not carbohydrates |
| Trans fat | As close to zero as possible | No safe level |
| Cholesterol | <200 mg/day (if LDL elevated) | Dietary cholesterol has less impact than saturated fat for most people |
| Sodium | <1,500-2,000 mg/day (heart failure); <2,300 mg/day (general CVD prevention) | DASH-style sodium restriction |
| Fiber | 25-30 g/day (7-13 g soluble) | Increase gradually to avoid GI discomfort |
| Omega-3 (EPA+DHA) | 500-1,000 mg/day | From fish or supplements |
| Potassium | 3,500-5,000 mg/day (if renal function adequate) | Part of DASH/Sodium reduction strategy |
| Alcohol | Women: <=1 drink/day; Men: <=2 drinks/day | Red wine not superior to other forms; binge drinking is harmful |

## Relationship to Conditions

- **Coronary artery disease (CAD):** The primary goal is LDL reduction. If statin therapy achieves less than 70 mg/dL LDL, dietary focus shifts to overall pattern (Mediterranean-style) rather than strict fat restriction. The PREDIMED trial demonstrated 30% reduction in major cardiovascular events with a Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra virgin olive oil or mixed nuts.
- **Heart failure:** Sodium restriction is the cornerstone. Fluid restriction (1.5-2 L/day) may be needed for patients with hyponatremia or volume overload. Cardiac cachexia (unintentional weight loss >7.5% in 6 months) requires caloric supplementation and sometimes omega-3 supplements. Appetite stimulation with small, frequent meals.
- **Atrial fibrillation:** Emerging evidence links obesity, sleep apnea, and alcohol excess to AF burden. Weight reduction of 10% or more can significantly reduce AF episodes. Limit alcohol to avoid "holiday heart" phenomenon.
- **Hypertension:** The DASH dietary pattern (high in fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy, whole grains; low in sodium, saturated fat, and sugar) combined with sodium reduction can lower systolic BP by 8-14 mmHg.
- **Post-cardiac surgery:** Protein needs increase to 1.2-1.5 g/kg/day for wound healing. Small, frequent meals if appetite is reduced. Continue sodium restriction. Resume cardiac diet pattern as soon as oral intake tolerates.
