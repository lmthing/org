# Mediterranean Diet

## Foods to Include

### Recommended (Daily Basis)

- **Extra virgin olive oil:** The primary fat source. 2-4 tablespoons per day. Rich in monounsaturated fat (oleic acid) and polyphenols (hydroxytyrosol, oleocanthal, oleuropein). Choose cold-pressed, first-harvest oils for highest polyphenol content. Use for cooking, dressing, and finishing.
- **Vegetables:** At least 5 servings per day (aim for 7+). Prioritize dark leafy greens (spinach, kale, chard), tomatoes, cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower), alliums (onions, garlic), and a variety of colors. Both raw and cooked.
- **Fruits:** 2-3 servings per day. Fresh, seasonal fruit as the primary dessert. Citrus, berries, apples, pears, figs, grapes, pomegranate, melon.
- **Whole grains:** At each meal. Sourdough bread (traditionally fermented), whole wheat pasta, bulgur, farro, couscous (whole wheat), rice (brown or white in traditional Mediterranean pattern), oats, barley. Portions are moderate — grains accompany the meal rather than dominate it.
- **Legumes and pulses:** At least 3 servings per week (traditionally daily in many Mediterranean cultures). Chickpeas (hummus, falafel), lentils (soups, stews), white beans, fava beans, black-eyed peas. Replace meat with legumes 3-4 times per week.
- **Nuts and seeds:** Daily, about 1-1.5 oz (a small handful). Almonds, walnuts, pistachios, pine nuts, sesame seeds (tahini). Used in cooking, as snacks, and in desserts.

### Recommended (Weekly Basis)

- **Fish and seafood:** At least 2-3 servings per week, prioritizing fatty fish. Salmon, sardines, anchovies, mackerel, tuna, sea bass, bream, octopus, squid. Preparation: grilled, baked, or stewed — not fried.
- **Poultry:** 2-3 servings per week. Grilled, baked, or stewed. Skinless when possible.
- **Eggs:** 2-4 per week. Prepared any style. Traditional Mediterranean breakfast often includes eggs with vegetables.
- **Dairy:** Moderate amounts. Primarily fermented forms: yogurt (full-fat or low-fat), feta cheese, goat cheese, ricotta, halloumi. 1-2 servings per day. Not a dominant protein source.
- **Potatoes:** Moderate portions, 2-3 times per week. Boiled, roasted, or in stews — not fried.

### Occasional

- **Red meat:** No more than 2 servings per month (traditional pattern). When consumed, small portions (3-4 oz) as a condiment or flavoring in stews rather than as a center-of-plate entree.
- **Sweets and desserts:** Limited to special occasions. Fresh fruit is the daily dessert. Traditional pastries (baklava, etc.) are reserved for celebrations.
- **Wine:** Moderate with meals (optional, not required). 1 glass per day for women, 1-2 for men. Red wine preferred for higher polyphenol content (resveratrol, quercetin). Non-drinkers should not start for health benefits.

## Foods to Avoid or Limit

- Processed meats (bacon, sausage, salami, hot dogs)
- Refined grains (white bread from non-sourdough, commercial pastries, sugary cereals)
- Sugar-sweetened beverages, fruit juices
- Fast food and ultra-processed convenience foods
- Industrial baked goods (cookies, cakes, donuts made with refined flour and added sugars)
- Margarine, butter (replaced by olive oil), and tropical oils
- Fried foods

## Meal Structure

The Mediterranean meal pattern is social, leisurely, and plant-centered:

- **Breakfast:** Whole grain bread with olive oil and tomato, or yogurt with fruit and nuts, or oatmeal with fruit. Coffee or tea.
- **Lunch (main meal in traditional pattern):** Large salad with olive oil dressing, legume soup or stew, whole grain bread, grilled vegetables, a small portion of fish or cheese. Fresh fruit for dessert.
- **Dinner:** Lighter than lunch. Vegetable-based soup, salad, omelet with vegetables, or a small portion of poultry/fish with vegetables. Whole grain bread with olive oil.
- **Snacks:** Fresh fruit, nuts, yogurt, hummus with vegetables. No packaged snack foods.
- **Meal timing:** Traditionally, the largest meal is at midday (2 PM), with a lighter evening meal (8-9 PM). This pattern may improve glucose metabolism compared to the Western pattern of a heavy dinner.

## Nutrient Targets

| Nutrient | Target | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Total fat | 35-40% of calories | Predominantly from olive oil (MUFA) and nuts |
| MUFA | 15-20% of calories | Primary fat source |
| Saturated fat | <8% of calories | Low due to minimal red meat and butter |
| Carbohydrate | 40-45% of calories | From whole grains, fruits, vegetables, legumes |
| Protein | 15-20% of calories | From fish, legumes, poultry, dairy, nuts — diverse sources |
| Fiber | 30-40 g/day | Naturally achieved through high plant intake |
| Omega-3 (EPA+DHA) | 500-1,000 mg/day | From fatty fish |
| Polyphenols | High (unmeasured) | From olive oil, wine, fruits, vegetables, herbs |
| Sodium | Moderate (~2,000 mg/day) | Naturally low due to minimal processed food |

## Relationship to Conditions

- **Cardiovascular disease (primary prevention):** The PREDIMED trial (n=7,447) demonstrated a 30% reduction in major cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, cardiovascular death) with a Mediterranean diet supplemented with either extra virgin olive oil or mixed nuts, compared to a reduced-fat control diet. This is the strongest dietary evidence for cardiovascular prevention.
- **Cardiovascular disease (secondary prevention):** The Lyon Diet Heart Study showed a 50-70% reduction in cardiac events and death in post-MI patients following a Mediterranean-style diet compared to a prudent Western diet. This benefit exceeded statin therapy alone.
- **Type 2 diabetes:** The Mediterranean diet improves glycemic control (HbA1c reduction of 0.3-0.5%) and reduces diabetes incidence by 20-25% in prospective cohorts. The high MUFA content improves insulin sensitivity compared to low-fat diets.
- **Cognitive decline and Alzheimer's disease:** Multiple prospective studies show 30-40% reduced risk of cognitive decline and Alzheimer's with greater Mediterranean diet adherence. The MIND diet (Mediterranean-DASH Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay) combines Mediterranean and DASH principles and showed 53% reduced Alzheimer's risk in the highest vs. lowest adherence quartile.
- **Cancer (colorectal):** The EPIC cohort and other studies show 20-30% reduced colorectal cancer risk with high Mediterranean diet adherence. Mechanisms include high fiber, polyphenols, and reduced red/processed meat intake.
- **Depression:** The SMILES trial and HELFIMED trial both demonstrated significant improvements in depression scores with Mediterranean diet intervention. The SUN cohort showed a 30-40% reduced risk of depression with highest adherence.
- **Metabolic syndrome: The Mediterranean diet addresses all five components of metabolic syndrome simultaneously — visceral obesity, elevated triglycerides, low HDL, elevated blood pressure, and elevated fasting glucose.
- **Longevity:** Multiple Blue Zone populations follow Mediterranean-adjacent dietary patterns. The combination of cardiovascular protection, anti-inflammatory effects, and metabolic regulation contributes to reduced all-cause mortality. A meta-analysis of prospective studies estimated 8-23% reduction in all-cause mortality per 2-point increase in Mediterranean diet adherence score.
