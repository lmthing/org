## Editorial Standards Assessment

A source's editorial quality is measured by its adherence to professional journalism standards.

### Key indicators

- **Published editorial guidelines**: Does the outlet have publicly stated editorial policies?
- **Correction policy**: Are errors acknowledged and corrected promptly? Is the correction process transparent?
- **Bylines and attribution**: Are authors named on stories? Are sources cited within articles?
- **Fact-checking process**: Does the outlet have an internal fact-checking process before publication?
- **Opinion vs news separation**: Is opinion content clearly separated from news reporting?
- **Headline accuracy**: Do headlines accurately reflect the content of the article?
- **Source diversity**: Does the outlet cite a range of sources, not just a narrow set of frequent commentators?

### Red flags

- No published editorial guidelines
- No visible correction policy
- Anonymous authors on news (not opinion) articles
- Headlines that misrepresent or sensationalise article content
- Opinion pieces presented as straight news without clear labelling
- Heavy reliance on single-source claims for major stories

### High-quality signals

- Member of a press council or journalistic standards body
- Regularly wins or is nominated for journalism awards
- Cited as a source by other reputable outlets
- Issues corrections prominently when errors are found
