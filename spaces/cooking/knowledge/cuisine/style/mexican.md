## Core philosophy

Mexican cuisine is built on **chilli complexity**, ancient staple grains (corn, beans), and layered technique. The holy trinity is chilli, tomato, and aromatics. Authentic Mexican cooking is not spicy for its own sake — it is nuanced, with dried chillies contributing fruity, smoky, earthy flavours distinct from raw heat.

## Chilli vocabulary

Understanding chilli character is essential. Fresh and dried are treated as separate ingredients.

| Fresh chilli | Heat (SHU) | Character |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| Poblano | 1,000–2,000 | Earthy, mild; becomes ancho when dried |
| Jalapeño | 2,500–8,000 | Grassy, bright; becomes chipotle when smoked/dried |
| Serrano | 10,000–23,000 | Bright, sharp |
| Habanero | 100,000–350,000 | Floral, tropical, intense |

| Dried chilli | Fresh equivalent | Character |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Ancho | Poblano | Raisin, chocolate, mild |
| Mulato | Poblano (dark) | Earthy, chocolate, tobacco |
| Pasilla | Chilaca | Berry, liquorice |
| Chipotle | Smoked jalapeño | Smoky, medium heat |
| Guajillo | Mirasol | Bright red, tart berry, mild |
| Arbol | Cayenne-type | Thin, fierce heat |

## Core techniques

### Toast and rehydrate dried chillies
1. Wipe seeds and veins if milder sauce is needed (seeds add bitterness, not heat).
2. Toast in a dry pan 30–60 seconds per side until fragrant (not black — that's bitter).
3. Soak in hot water 20–30 min, drain. Reserve soaking liquid for diluting sauces.

### Make masa properly
- **Masa harina** (nixtamalised corn flour): add warm water until dough holds without cracking or sticking (test ball should flatten without cracks at edges).
- Rest dough 20 min covered — hydration continues.
- Press tortillas between plastic sheets to prevent sticking. Thickness: 2mm for tortillas, 5mm for sopes and gorditas.
- Cook on a dry comal or cast iron at medium-high; first side 30–45 s (edges lift), flip, 1 min, flip again for puffing.

### Mole foundations
Mole negro (Oaxaca) layers 20+ ingredients: multiple dried chillies, chocolate, tomato, tomatillo, charred onion, plantain, raisins, spices, stale bread or tortilla (thickener). Toast each component separately; blend in stages. A proper mole requires 3–6 hours.

## Regional distinctions

| Region | Signature | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Oaxaca | Mole negro, tlayudas, chapulines | Seven moles; Oaxacan cheese (quesillo) |
| Yucatán | Cochinita pibil, sopa de lima | Achiote (annatto), sour orange, banana-leaf cooking |
| Veracruz | Huachinango a la Veracruzana, totonaba | Spanish-influenced; olives, capers, tomatoes |
| Mexico City | Tacos de canasta, barbacoa, carnitas | Street food capital; lamb, beef, pork |

## Pantry essentials

- **Epazote**: pungent herb essential in black bean cooking (reduces flatulence and adds flavour).
- **Lard (manteca)**: traditional fat for refried beans, tamales, and carnitas. Crisco is a poor substitute.
- **Mexican oregano**: different species from Mediterranean oregano — more citrus, less pine. Not interchangeable.
- **Crema**: Mexican soured cream, lower fat and tangier than sour cream. Crème fraîche is the closest substitute.
