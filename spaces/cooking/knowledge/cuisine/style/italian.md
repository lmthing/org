## Core philosophy

Italian cooking centres on **few, high-quality ingredients** treated simply. A dish with four ingredients done perfectly beats one with twelve done adequately. Mise en place is always ready before heat meets pan.

## Key ingredients

- **Pasta**: fresh egg pasta (Emilia-Romagna), dried durum wheat semolina (South). Match shape to sauce weight: ribbons → rich meat sauces; tubes → chunky vegetables; thin strands → olive oil and seafood.
- **Olive oil**: finishing oil (DOP extra-virgin) is not the same as cooking oil. Use a neutral-tasting virgin oil for high-heat sautéing; reserve EVOO for finishing.
- **San Marzano tomatoes**: lower acidity, thicker flesh than Roma. Non-negotiable for Neapolitan pizza sauce. Crush by hand — no blending.
- **Parmigiano-Reggiano vs Pecorino Romano**: Parmigiano is nutty and subtle; Pecorino is sharp and salty. Carbonara uses Pecorino; Bolognese uses Parmigiano.

## Techniques

- **Pasta water**: aggressively salted (tastes of the sea). Reserve a cup before draining — the starch emulsifies fat-based sauces.
- **Al dente**: remove pasta 1–2 min before package time. It finishes cooking in the sauce pan over low heat with pasta water.
- **Soffritto**: sweat onion, celery, carrot gently in olive oil (15–20 min, no browning) as the flavour base for most braises and ragù.
- **Pizza dough**: 24–72 h cold ferment builds flavour. Bake at the highest oven setting; a preheated steel or stone is mandatory.

## Regional distinctions

| Region | Signature dishes | Notes |
|--------|-----------------|-------|
| Campania (Naples) | Pizza Margherita, Ragù alla Napoletana | Tomato-forward, mozzarella di bufala |
| Emilia-Romagna | Tagliatelle al Ragù, Tortellini, Lasagne | Egg pasta, butter over oil, long slow braises |
| Lazio (Rome) | Carbonara, Cacio e Pepe, Amatriciana | Guanciale (not pancetta), Pecorino Romano |
| Lombardy (Milan) | Risotto alla Milanese, Ossobuco | Butter-based, saffron, gremolata finish |
| Sicily | Caponata, Pasta alla Norma, Arancini | Arab and Spanish influences, eggplant, pine nuts |

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Adding cream to carbonara — the creaminess comes from emulsified egg yolk and pasta water, not cream.
- Salting pasta water with too little salt — it should taste noticeably salty.
- Over-saucing pasta — Italians coat pasta, they don't drown it.
- Boiling risotto — it should simmer gently; continuous stirring releases starch gradually.
