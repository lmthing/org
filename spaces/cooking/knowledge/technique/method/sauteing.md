title: Sautéing
description: High-heat dry-pan cooking for quick proteins, vegetables, and sauce building
---

## Principle

Sautéing (from French *sauter*, "to jump") uses **high heat and a small amount of fat** to cook quickly while developing browning on the surface. The technique is about control of heat, timing, and motion.

## The pan matters

- **Stainless steel**: excellent for fond development (brown bits = sauce). Requires preheating and more fat.
- **Carbon steel**: similar to stainless; seasons over time; lighter than cast iron.
- **Non-stick**: no fond, but useful for eggs and delicate fish where sticking is the risk.
- **Cast iron**: excellent heat retention, good for searing; too slow for wok-style sauté.

## Heat management

1. **Preheat empty**: 60–90 seconds on medium-high before adding fat. Water drop should bead and dance (Leidenfrost effect) — the pan is ready.
2. **Add fat**: it should shimmer and flow easily, not smoke immediately. Smoking = overheated → bitter.
3. **Don't move too soon**: for proteins, let the Maillard crust form. It will release naturally when ready — forcing it pulls the surface off.
4. **Vegetables**: keep moving for even cooking; don't let them sit long enough to steam.

## Fond and pan sauces

The brown bits stuck to the pan after sautéing protein are the **fond** — concentrated caramelised proteins and sugars.

To deglaze:
1. Remove protein; keep residual fat.
2. Add aromatics (shallot, garlic) if making a sauce; sweat 30 sec.
3. Add liquid (wine, stock, citrus juice); bring to a boil.
4. Scrape up the fond — it dissolves into the liquid.
5. Reduce until slightly syrupy; mount with cold butter (beurre monté) off heat for gloss.

## Timing by ingredient

| Ingredient | Heat | Time |
|-----------|------|------|
| Shrimp (large) | High | 1–2 min per side |
| Chicken breast (butterflied) | Medium-high | 4–5 min per side |
| Steak (2cm) | High | 2–3 min per side + rest |
| Mushrooms | High | 3–5 min, no stirring until golden |
| Asparagus | Medium-high | 3–4 min toss |
| Spinach/greens | High | 60–90 sec, toss constantly |
| Scallops | High | 90 sec per side, do not move |

## Common mistakes

- **Wet proteins**: always pat dry. Surface moisture creates steam, inhibiting browning.
- **Cold pan**: protein sticks and steams instead of searing.
- **Overcrowding**: pieces touching release steam. Leave space or cook in batches.
- **Moving too much**: proteins need time undisturbed to form a crust and release.
