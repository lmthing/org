## Core philosophy

French classical cuisine is built on **technique and precision**. Every stock, sauce, and cut follows a reproducible method. The goal is to extract maximum flavour from each ingredient through controlled heat and timing.

## The mother sauces

Master these five; every classical French sauce is a derivative.

| Sauce | Base | Key derivatives |
|-------|------|----------------|
| Béchamel | Milk + white roux | Mornay (+ cheese), Soubise (+ onion) |
| Velouté | White stock + blonde roux | Allemande, Suprême, Normande |
| Espagnole | Brown stock + brown roux | Demi-glace, Bordelaise, Chasseur |
| Hollandaise | Egg yolks + clarified butter + acid | Béarnaise (+ tarragon), Maltaise |
| Sauce Tomat | Tomatoes + stock | Provençale, Portugaise |

## Mirepoix and aromatics

- **Mirepoix**: 2 parts onion : 1 part carrot : 1 part celery — the universal flavour base for stocks and braises.
- **Bouquet garni**: bay leaf, thyme, parsley stems bundled in leek or cheesecloth. Always remove before serving.
- **Mise en place**: everything prepped, measured, and within reach before the first burner lights.

## Stocks (fonds)

| Stock | Bones | Notes |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Fond blanc (veal) | Veal, unroasted | 6–8 h; basis for velouté and demi-glace |
| Fond brun | Beef/veal, roasted | 8–12 h; caramelised mirepoix |
| Fond de volaille | Chicken, unroasted | 3–4 h |
| Fumet de poisson | Fish bones (not oily) | 20–25 min only; longer = bitter |

Stocks are never salted — they reduce and concentrate; season at the sauce stage.

## Classical cuts

- **Brunoise**: 3mm dice — garnish, fine sauces
- **Julienne**: 3mm × 3mm × 5cm batons — sautéed vegetables, stir-ins
- **Chiffonade**: thin ribbons of herbs/leaves — finishing garnish
- **Tourné**: 7-sided barrel shape — classical vegetable presentation

## Common French techniques

- **Sauté**: very high heat, minimal fat, constant motion — for tender proteins and mushrooms.
- **Braise (braiser)**: brown first, then slow-cook in liquid in a covered vessel — for collagen-rich cuts.
- **En papillote**: bake sealed in parchment — fish and vegetables steam in their own moisture.
- **Beurre monté**: whisk cold butter into a warm (not hot) liquid to create an emulsified sauce. Temperature must stay below 82°C or the emulsion breaks.
