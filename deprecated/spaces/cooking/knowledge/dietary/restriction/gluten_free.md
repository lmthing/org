## Definition

Gluten is a protein complex (**glutenin + gliadin**) found in wheat, barley, rye, and their derivatives. A gluten-free diet avoids all sources of these grains. For coeliac disease, even trace cross-contamination (< 20 ppm) causes immune damage.

## Disqualifying ingredients (flag these)

| Ingredient | Gluten source | Safe substitute |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| All-purpose / bread / cake flour | Wheat | Rice flour, oat flour (certified GF), almond flour, tapioca, buckwheat |
| Pasta (conventional) | Wheat semolina | Rice pasta, chickpea pasta, lentil pasta, corn pasta |
| Couscous | Wheat | Millet, quinoa |
| Breadcrumbs | Wheat | GF breadcrumbs, almond meal, crushed GF crackers |
| Soy sauce | Wheat (most brands) | Tamari (most tamari is wheat-free; check label), coconut aminos |
| Barley (incl. malt, barley vinegar) | Barley | Omit or substitute apple cider vinegar |
| Beer | Barley/wheat | GF beer, cider, stock |
| Rye bread / rye flour | Rye | GF rye-style blends |
| Seitan / wheat gluten | Pure gluten | Tofu, tempeh, jackfruit |
| Many pre-made spice blends | May contain wheat flour as anti-caking | Check label; use single spices |

## Hidden gluten traps

- **Oats**: naturally gluten-free but frequently cross-contaminated in processing. Use certified gluten-free oats.
- **Modified food starch**: usually corn-derived (safe) but can be wheat-derived — check source.
- **Malt flavouring / malt extract**: barley-derived unless labelled "corn malt". Avoid.
- **Sauces and gravies**: many use wheat flour as thickener. Use cornstarch or arrowroot instead.
- **Bouillon cubes and stock powders**: frequently contain wheat.
- **Baking powder**: a small number contain wheat starch — check label.
- **Lipstick / medications**: relevant for coeliac patients; outside cooking scope.

## Baking without gluten

Gluten provides the **elastic network** that traps gas bubbles and gives baked goods structure. Without it, baked goods crumble or are dense unless compensated:

- **Binding agents**: xanthan gum (¼ tsp per cup of GF flour), psyllium husk, flax egg. Essential for yeast breads and cakes.
- **Blended flours**: single GF flours rarely work well alone. A common blend: 40% rice flour + 30% tapioca + 20% potato starch + 10% sorghum.
- **More moisture**: GF flours absorb water differently. Most GF batters are wetter than conventional.
- **Longer rest**: allow GF batter to rest 10–15 min before baking; starches hydrate fully and reduce grittiness.
- **Lower bake temperature, longer time**: GF baked goods brown faster on the outside while staying raw inside. Tent with foil if over-browning.

## Cross-contamination (for coeliac)

- Dedicated equipment (boards, colanders, toaster) if the household is shared.
- Always cook GF items first in a shared kitchen session.
- Pasta water from wheat pasta contaminates a cooking pot for GF use — use a separate pot.
- Shared fryer oil is not safe — crumbs from breaded items remain.

## Fit-check rules

A recipe **fails** the gluten-free check if any disqualifying ingredient is present without a noted GF substitute. For coeliac severity, also flag any certified-GF labelling requirements on oats, soy sauce, or oat milk used.
