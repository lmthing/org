## Principle

Braising is **combination cooking**: sear first (Maillard browning), then cook slowly in liquid (80–95°C), covered. The low sustained heat converts collagen in connective tissue to gelatin, creating silky, rich texture impossible with dry-heat methods. The cooking liquid becomes the sauce.

## Why it works

- **Collagen → gelatin**: begins converting at ~70°C; full conversion at 80–85°C sustained for 2–4 hours depending on cut thickness.
- **Liquid level**: braises are not fully submerged (that's poaching). Liquid should come ⅓–½ up the sides. Steam inside the pot handles the rest.
- **Low, steady temperature**: 150–165°C oven, lid tight. Bubbling aggressively = overcooked, stringy meat.

## Ideal cuts

- **Beef**: short rib, chuck, brisket, oxtail, cheek
- **Pork**: shoulder, belly, neck, cheek
- **Lamb**: shoulder, shank, neck
- **Veal**: shank (ossobuco), cheek
- **Chicken**: thigh, drumstick (not breast — collagen-poor)

## Step-by-step

1. **Dry and season** the protein generously with salt. Surface moisture is the enemy of browning.
2. **Sear in batches** in a heavy oven-safe pot (Dutch oven) over high heat. Don't crowd. Each side needs 3–4 min undisturbed. Remove and set aside.
3. **Sweat aromatics** (mirepoix / onion / soffritto) in the rendered fat, medium heat, 8–10 min until soft.
4. **Deglaze** with wine or stock; scrape the fond. Reduce by half.
5. **Add braising liquid** (stock, tomatoes, or a combination) to ⅓–½ up the sides.
6. **Nestle the protein** back in; bring to a bare simmer on the stovetop, then transfer to a 150–160°C oven, covered.
7. **Cook low and slow**: check after 1 h for braising liquid level. Total time: 1.5 h (chicken), 2.5–3 h (pork shoulder), 3–4 h (beef short rib), 3.5–5 h (brisket).
8. **Test doneness**: fork should slide in with very little resistance. For pull-apart meats, internal temp 88–93°C.
9. **Strain and reduce the braising liquid** into a sauce. Skim fat. Season at this stage, not before — the liquid concentrates as it reduces.

## Tips

- **Always sear in the braising vessel** if possible — the fond is flavour.
- **Oven > stovetop**: oven heat is all-around and more stable. Stovetop braises scorch on the bottom.
- **Braise ahead**: collagen-rich dishes improve overnight in the fridge. Reheat gently to serve.
- **Acid**: a splash of wine, tomatoes, or vinegar balances the richness of the gelatin. Don't skip.
