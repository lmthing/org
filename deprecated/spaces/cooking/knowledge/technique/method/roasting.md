title: Roasting
description: Dry-heat oven cooking for large cuts, root vegetables, and whole birds

## Principle

Roasting uses **dry convective heat** (typically 160–240°C) to drive Maillard browning on exposed surfaces while gently heating the interior through conduction. The key variables are:

- **Temperature**: high heat (220°C+) for fast browning and crisp skin; low-and-slow (130–160°C) for even doneness in thick cuts.
- **Fat**: coat surfaces to promote even browning and moisture retention. Pat protein dry first — surface moisture steams rather than browns.
- **Resting**: allow juices to redistribute after removing from heat. Large cuts (whole chicken, prime rib): 15–30 min. Steaks: 5–8 min.

## Temperature guide

| Protein | Internal target | Notes |
|---------|----------------|-------|
| Chicken breast | 74°C (165°F) | Safe; rests to ~76°C |
| Chicken thigh | 82°C (180°F) | Collagen converts; more forgiving |
| Pork loin | 63°C (145°F) | USDA safe; slight pink is fine |
| Pork shoulder | 88–93°C (190–200°F) | Pull-apart collagen conversion |
| Beef rare | 52°C (125°F) | Deep red |
| Beef medium | 60°C (140°F) | Pink throughout |
| Beef well | 71°C (160°F) | Grey throughout |
| Lamb rack | 57°C (135°F) | Medium-rare; carryover to ~60°C |

Always measure at the **thickest part**, away from bone (bone conducts heat unevenly).

## Technique notes

- **Elevated roasting rack**: keeps the bottom from steaming. Essential for crispy skin.
- **Basting**: adds moisture and flavour but opens the oven (drops temperature). Trade-off depends on recipe.
- **Searing first vs. reverse sear**: conventional sear-then-roast gives a crust; reverse sear (roast low first, then sear) gives more even edge-to-edge doneness with equal crust. Reverse sear recommended for thick steaks (≥ 4cm).
- **Carryover cooking**: temperature rises 3–8°C after removal from oven. Pull proteins early; rest before serving.

## Vegetables

- **Cut size matters**: uniform size = uniform doneness. Cut root vegetables 2–3cm thick.
- **High heat** (210–220°C) caramelises natural sugars. Low heat steams rather than roasts.
- **Don't crowd the pan**: overlapping pieces steam each other. Single layer, with space.
- **Fat and toss before**: 1–2 tbsp oil per 500g vegetables; coat evenly.
- **Timing by density**: hard roots (parsnip, potato, carrot): 35–45 min. Softer (zucchini, asparagus): 12–20 min.
