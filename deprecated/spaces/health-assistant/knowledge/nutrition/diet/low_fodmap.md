# Low FODMAP Diet

## Foods to Include

### Phase 1: Elimination (2-6 weeks)

During the elimination phase, only low-FODMAP foods are allowed. This is not a permanent diet — it is a diagnostic tool to identify which fermentable carbohydrates trigger symptoms.

**Low-FODMAP foods by category:**

- **Proteins (all are low-FODMAP):** Chicken, beef, pork, fish, eggs, tofu (firm), tempeh. Unprocessed, unmarinated meats.
- **Grains and starches:** Rice (white and brown), quinoa, oats (up to 1/2 cup per serving), potatoes, sweet potatoes (1/2 cup), sourdough bread (wheat-based, fermented long enough to degrade fructans), rice noodles, gluten-free pasta, polenta, corn tortillas.
- **Vegetables:** Bell peppers, carrots, cucumber, zucchini, spinach, kale, tomatoes, green beans, eggplant, bok choy, choy sum, lettuce, radishes, potatoes, parsnips, turnips, squash (butternut, up to 1/4 cup).
- **Fruits:** Bananas (firm/slightly green), blueberries, strawberries, kiwi, oranges, mandarins, grapes, cantaloupe, honeydew, pineapple, papaya, passionfruit, rhubarb, lemons, limes.
- **Dairy alternatives:** Lactose-free milk, almond milk, rice milk, coconut milk (canned, up to 1/2 cup), hard cheeses (cheddar, Swiss, parmesan, brie, camembert — lactose is fermented during aging), butter.
- **Sweeteners:** Maple syrup, sugar (sucrose), glucose, rice malt syrup, stevia, monk fruit. Avoid honey, agave, high-fructose corn syrup, sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol, and isomalt.

## Foods to Avoid (Elimination Phase)

### High-FODMAP Categories

FODMAP is an acronym for:
- **F**ermentable
- **O**ligosaccharides (fructans and galacto-oligosaccharides/GOS)
- **D**isaccharides (lactose)
- **M**onosaccharides (excess fructose)
- **A**nd
- **P**olyols (sorbitol, mannitol)

| FODMAP Group | High-FODMAP Foods to Avoid |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| **Fructans** | Wheat-based products (bread, pasta, cereals), rye, barley, onions, garlic, shallots, leeks, artichokes, asparagus, beetroot, Brussels sprouts, cabbage (savoy), fennel, chicory root, inulin, dandelion greens |
| **GOS** | Legumes (chickpeas, lentils, kidney beans, black beans, baked beans), soy milk (made from whole soybeans), cashews, pistachios |
| **Lactose** | Cow's milk, ice cream, soft unripened cheeses (ricotta, cottage cheese, cream cheese), yogurt, custard, evaporated milk, condensed milk |
| **Excess Fructose** | Honey, agave nectar, apples, pears, watermelon, mangoes, cherries, figs, high-fructose corn syrup, fruit juices (apple, pear, mango), dried fruits (raisins, dates, figs) |
| **Polyols** | Sugar-free gums and mints (sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol), stone fruits (peaches, plums, nectarines, apricots — sorbitol), apples and pears (contain both excess fructose and sorbitol), mushrooms (mannitol), cauliflower (mannitol in large amounts), snow peas |

### Hidden FODMAPs

- **Garlic and onion powder:** Present in nearly all commercially prepared foods, sauces, dressings, and spice blends. This is the most common reason for elimination failure. Read all labels carefully.
- **Inulin/chicory root fiber:** Added to many "high-fiber" and "probiotic" yogurts, granola bars, and protein bars as a prebiotic. It is pure fructan and will trigger symptoms in fructan-sensitive individuals.
- **Honey:** Used as a sweetener in many "natural" and "health" products.
- **Cashew and pistachio milks:** Higher in GOS than almond or rice milk.

## Meal Structure

### Elimination Phase

- **3 meals + 1-2 snacks per day.** Regular meal timing prevents excessive fermentation from large meals.
- **Portion awareness:** Some foods are low-FODMAP in small portions but high-FODMAP in larger portions (e.g., 1/4 avocado is low-FODMAP but 1/2 avocado is high-FODMAP due to sorbitol). Use a FODMAP-specific serving guide (Monash University FODMAP app is the gold standard).
- **Meal composition:** Build each meal around a low-FODMAP protein, a low-FODMAP grain/starch, and low-FODMAP vegetables. Add fruit or a low-FODMAP dairy alternative as a side.
- **Garlic/onion substitute:** Use garlic-infused olive oil (fructans are not oil-soluble, so the flavor transfers without the FODMAPs), the green part of spring onions/scallions, chives, or asafoetida powder (hing) for onion-like flavor.

### Reintroduction Phase (after 2-6 weeks if symptoms improved)

Reintroduce FODMAP groups one at a time, in increasing portions, over 3 days per group:
1. Test lactose (milk, then yogurt)
2. Test fructans (wheat bread, then garlic)
3. Test GOS (canned chickpeas, then hummus)
4. Test excess fructose (honey, then mango)
5. Test polyols (sorbitol-containing candy, then stone fruit)

Test one group at a time with a 3-day washout between groups if symptoms recur. Document the threshold dose and the symptoms for each group.

### Personalization Phase

Once trigger FODMAPs and thresholds are identified, liberalize the diet by including tolerated FODMAPs at tolerated doses. The goal is the least restrictive diet that controls symptoms. Most patients can tolerate 2-3 FODMAP groups at moderate portions.

## Nutrient Targets

| Nutrient | Consideration | Notes |
|----------|--------------|-------|
| Calcium | 1,000-1,300 mg/day | If dairy is eliminated, ensure adequate calcium from lactose-free dairy, fortified plant milks, or supplements |
| Fiber | 25-30 g/day (from low-FODMAP sources) | Oats (up to 1/2 cup/serving), rice, quinoa, potatoes, kiwi, strawberries. Do not use wheat bran or inulin supplements. |
| Iron | Standard intake | Iron-fortified cereals and breads may contain wheat. Use rice-based or oat-based alternatives. |
| Vitamin D | 600-800 IU/day | Limited fortified dairy intake during elimination may require supplementation |
| B-vitamins | Adequate intake | Reduced whole grain intake may lower B-vitamin intake; consider a multivitamin |

## Relationship to Conditions

- **Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS):** The low-FODMAP diet has the strongest evidence base for IBS symptom management, with response rates of 50-80% across clinical trials. It is recommended as a second-line intervention after first-line IBS dietary advice (regular meals, adequate fiber, limited caffeine/alcohol/fatty foods).
- **Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO):** Often overlaps with IBS. The low-FODMAP diet reduces the substrate available for bacterial fermentation in the small intestine. It is used as an adjunct to antibiotic therapy (rifaximin), not a replacement. After successful SIBO treatment, gradual FODMAP reintroduction is essential to prevent dietary over-restriction.
- **Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD):** The low-FODMAP diet may help manage functional symptoms in IBD patients in remission, but it is not a treatment for IBD inflammation. Caution: already malnourished IBD patients should not further restrict their diet without dietitian supervision.
- **Endometriosis:** Some women with endometriosis report GI symptom improvement on a low-FODMAP diet, though evidence is limited. The mechanism may relate to shared visceral hypersensitivity pathways.
- **Non-celiac gluten sensitivity:** Some patients with NCGS may actually be sensitive to fructans in wheat rather than gluten itself. A low-FODMAP trial can distinguish between fructan and gluten sensitivity.
