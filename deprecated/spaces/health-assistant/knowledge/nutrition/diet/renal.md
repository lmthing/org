# Renal Diet

## Foods to Include

### Recommended

- **High-quality protein (controlled portions):** Egg whites, skinless chicken breast, fish (cod, tilapia, tuna). For non-dialysis CKD stages 3-4, limit to 0.6-0.8 g/kg/day. For hemodialysis patients, increase to 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day due to dialytic protein losses.
- **Low-potassium fruits:** Apples, berries (blueberries, strawberries, cranberries), grapes, peaches, plums, pineapple, watermelon (1 cup servings).
- **Low-potassium vegetables:** Green beans, cabbage, cauliflower, cucumber, eggplant, lettuce, onions, peppers, zucchini. Leaching (soaking cut vegetables in warm water for 2+ hours) reduces potassium content by 50-75%.
- **Low-phosphorus grains:** White rice, white bread, pasta, rice cereal, corn tortillas. Phosphorus from plant sources is less bioavailable (40-60%) than from animal sources or phosphate additives (90-100%).
- **Healthy fats:** Olive oil, canola oil, avocado (small portions — watch potassium). Provide calories without protein, potassium, or phosphorus load.
- **Fresh or frozen unprocessed foods:** Avoid phosphate additives (sodium phosphate, phosphoric acid) in processed meats, colas, and frozen meals. Additive phosphorus is nearly 100% absorbed versus 40-60% for naturally occurring phosphorus.

## Foods to Avoid or Limit

### High Potassium (limit if serum K >5.0 mEq/L)

- Bananas, oranges, orange juice, cantaloupe, honeydew, dried fruits (raisins, dates, prunes)
- Potatoes, tomatoes, tomato sauce, sweet potatoes, winter squash, spinach, beet greens, avocados
- Beans and legumes (kidney, black, pinto), lentils, hummus
- Yogurt, chocolate, molasses, salt substitutes ( potassium chloride)

### High Phosphorus (limit if serum phosphorus >4.5 mg/dL)

- Dairy products: milk, cheese, yogurt, ice cream (limit to 4 oz milk or 1 oz cheese per day)
- Nuts and seeds: almonds, peanuts, sunflower seeds, peanut butter
- Whole grains: bran, wheat germ, oatmeal (moderate — portion-dependent)
- Organ meats, sardines, colas with phosphoric acid, processed cheese, flavored waters with phosphate additives
- Baking powder, biscuit mix (contain sodium aluminum phosphate)

### High Sodium (limit to less than 2,000 mg/day)

- Processed meats, canned soups, frozen dinners, fast food, soy sauce, pickled foods

## Meal Structure

- **3 meals per day** with optional snack if needed for caloric adequacy. Avoid large, infrequent meals which contribute to electrolyte spikes.
- **Protein distribution:** Spread protein intake evenly across meals rather than concentrating at dinner. This improves nitrogen utilization and reduces uremic load per meal.
- **Portion control:** Use standard measuring tools. A typical renal meal plate: 1/4 plate lean protein (3 oz cooked), 1/4 plate low-potassium starch (1/2 cup rice or pasta), 1/2 plate low-potassium vegetables.
- **Fluid management:** For patients with fluid retention or on dialysis, limit to urine output + 500-1000 mL/day. Count all liquids including soups, ice, popsicles, and water-rich fruits.

## Nutrient Targets (Stage 3-5 CKD, non-dialysis)

| Nutrient | Target | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Protein | 0.6-0.8 g/kg/day | Plant-predominant protein may reduce hyperfiltration |
| Potassium | 2,000-3,000 mg/day | Adjust based on serum levels; liberalize if K <4.0 |
| Phosphorus | 800-1,000 mg/day | Focus on avoiding phosphate additives |
| Sodium | <2,000 mg/day | BP and fluid control |
| Calcium | 800-1,000 mg/day | Balance with phosphorus; avoid excess with calcifications |
| Fluid | Urine output + 500-1000 mL | Tighten if edema or hyponatremia |
| Calories | 25-35 kcal/kg/day | Prevent protein-energy wasting |
| Iron | Supplement as needed | ESA therapy increases iron demand |

For hemodialysis patients: increase protein to 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day, potassium and phosphorus limits may be liberalized slightly based on labs, ensure adequate caloric intake on non-dialysis days.

## Relationship to Conditions

- **Diabetes and CKD:** The renal diet must be combined with carbohydrate consistency. SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) are now standard of care for diabetic CKD (eGFR >=20) regardless of glycemic control — they reduce CKD progression independently of glucose lowering.
- **Hypertension and CKD:** Sodium restriction to less than 2,000 mg/day augments antihypertensive efficacy, particularly ACE inhibitors and ARBs. DASH diet is often too high in potassium, phosphorus, and magnesium for CKD patients — a modified version is needed.
- **Cardiovascular disease:** CKD patients have the highest cardiovascular risk of any population. Manage dyslipidemia, limit saturated fat, and ensure adequate omega-3 intake (EPA/DHA). Avoid phosphate additives which are independently associated with vascular calcification.
- **Bone disease (CKD-MBD):** Phosphorus retention drives FGF23 and PTH elevation. Dietary phosphate restriction is the first-line intervention. Vitamin D supplementation (calcitriol or analogs) should be guided by PTH, calcium, and phosphorus levels — not 25(OH)D alone.
