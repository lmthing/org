## Symptom Patterns (Call 911 Immediately)

### Cardiovascular
- Chest pain or pressure (especially with radiation to arm, jaw, back)
- Sudden severe difficulty breathing
- Heart rate >150 or <40 with symptoms
- Suspected cardiac arrest (unresponsive, no pulse)

### Neurological
- Sudden severe headache ("thunderclap" or "worst headache of my life")
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side (face, arm, leg)
- Sudden difficulty speaking or understanding speech (slurred speech, confusion)
- Sudden vision loss in one or both eyes
- Seizure (new onset or prolonged >5 min)
- Loss of consciousness

### Respiratory
- Severe difficulty breathing (cannot speak in full sentences)
- Bluish lips or face (cyanosis)
- Choking with inability to breathe or speak

### Hemorrhagic
- Uncontrolled bleeding (spurting or soaking through bandages)
- Coughing up large amounts of blood
- Vomiting blood (hematemesis)
- Black tarry stools with dizziness or fainting

### Allergic/Anaphylaxis
- Throat swelling, difficulty swallowing
- Rapid-onset widespread hives with breathing difficulty
- Anaphylaxis: rapid swelling, wheezing, hypotension, collapse

### Mental Health
- Active suicidal ideation with plan and intent
- Homicidal ideation
- Acute psychosis with safety risk

### Other
- Severe burn (face, hands, feet, genitals, or >10% body surface)
- Suspected poisoning or overdose (unconscious or altered mental status)
- Severe dehydration (no urine output >8h, extreme thirst, confusion)
- High fever (>104°F/40°C) with altered mental status or stiff neck

## Time-to-Treatment

- **Target: Immediate** — minutes matter
- Call 911 — do not attempt to drive yourself
- Begin CPR if unresponsive and no pulse
- Use epinephrine auto-injector for anaphylaxis if available

## Care Setting

**Emergency Room via ambulance** — paramedics can begin treatment en route

## Red-Flag Escalation Criteria

ANY of the above symptoms = immediate 911 call. Do not delay. Do not wait to see if symptoms improve. Do not attempt self-treatment.
