## Symptom Patterns (Seek Care Within 24 Hours)

### General
- Persistent high fever (>103°F/39.4°C) not responding to antipyretics
- Worsening infection signs (increasing redness, swelling, warmth, streaking)
- Persistent vomiting >24 hours (unable to keep fluids down)
- Persistent diarrhea >48 hours with dehydration signs
- New or worsening severe pain (not controlled by OTC medication)

### Cardiovascular
- New-onset palpitations without chest pain
- Rapid heart rate at rest (>120 bpm) without obvious cause
- New or significantly worsening leg swelling (edema)

### Respiratory
- Worsening shortness of breath with mild exertion
- New persistent cough with fever
- Wheezing not relieved by rescue inhaler (if asthmatic)

### Abdominal
- Severe abdominal pain with nausea/vomiting
- Blood in stool (red or black/tarry)
- Persistent abdominal pain >6 hours

### Neurological
- New severe dizziness or vertigo
- Sudden hearing loss
- Worsening headache over days
- New weakness (not sudden onset — that's emergency)

### Musculoskeletal
- Suspected fracture (significant swelling, deformity, inability to bear weight)
- Deep or large laceration requiring stitches
- Animal or human bite
- Suspected deep vein thrombosis (calf swelling, pain, warmth)

### Mental Health
- Suicidal ideation without active plan
- Severe anxiety or panic not responding to usual coping
- Acute substance use concern

## Time-to-Treatment

- **Target: within 24 hours**
- Urgent care center, walk-in clinic, or same-day primary care appointment
- If symptoms worsen to emergency level, escalate immediately

## Care Setting

**Urgent Care Center** or **Same-day primary care appointment**

## Red-Flag Escalation Criteria

Escalate to emergency if any of these develop:
- Chest pain develops
- Breathing becomes difficult
- Neurological symptoms worsen (weakness, speech changes)
- Fever rises above 104°F/40°C
- Mental status changes (confusion, drowsiness)
- Bleeding becomes uncontrolled
