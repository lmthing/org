# Drug-Condition Interactions

## Interaction Mechanisms

Drug-condition interactions (also called disease-drug or condition-drug interactions) occur when a patient's underlying medical condition alters the pharmacokinetics or pharmacodynamics of a drug, or when the drug worsens the underlying condition. Unlike drug-drug interactions, these are not about two substances interacting — they are about a patient's physiology modifying drug safety and efficacy.

### Pharmacokinetic Alterations by Organ Dysfunction

- **Renal impairment:** Reduces glomerular filtration and tubular secretion, leading to accumulation of renally cleared drugs and active metabolites. The GFR decline is not linear with creatinine because muscle mass also declines with age and illness.
- **Hepatic impairment:** Reduces first-pass metabolism, phase I oxidation (CYP), phase II conjugation (glucuronidation, sulfation), and biliary excretion. Child-Pugh classification guides dose adjustments.
- **Cardiac failure:** Reduced perfusion to kidneys and liver, plus third-spacing of fluid, alters drug distribution and clearance. Edematous patients may need higher loading doses but lower maintenance doses.
- **GI disease:** Malabsorption (celiac, Crohn's, short bowel syndrome) can reduce drug absorption. Gastric bypass surgery reduces the absorptive surface and may bypass the primary dissolution site for extended-release formulations.

### Pharmacodynamic Sensitization

Some conditions make patients more vulnerable to specific drug effects regardless of pharmacokinetics:
- Seizure disorders are worsened by drugs that lower the seizure threshold (bupropion, tramadol, high-dose penicillins).
- Heart failure is worsened by drugs that cause sodium and water retention (NSAIDs, thiazolidinediones, pregabalin).
- Respiratory depression is amplified in COPD/sleep apnea by opioids and benzodiazepines.
- Glaucoma may be worsened by anticholinergic drugs (angle-closure risk) or systemic corticosteroids (elevated intraocular pressure).

## Severity Levels

| Level | Criteria | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **Minor** | The condition causes a small, predictable change in drug handling. The drug can generally be used without adjustment but with standard monitoring. | Use the drug at standard doses. Routine monitoring. |
| **Moderate** | The condition significantly alters drug clearance or the drug has a known potential to worsen the condition. Dose adjustment or enhanced monitoring is required. | Adjust dose based on organ function (eGFR, Child-Pugh class). Schedule monitoring within 1-2 weeks of initiation. Consider therapeutic alternatives. |
| **Severe** | The drug poses a serious risk of worsening the condition or the condition creates a high risk of drug toxicity. Use only if no alternatives exist and with close supervision. | Avoid if alternatives exist. If required, use the lowest effective dose with intensified monitoring (labs, clinical assessment). Document clinical rationale. |
| **Contraindicated** | The drug is known to cause serious harm in patients with the condition, or the condition makes safe dosing impossible. | Do not use. Find alternatives. |

## Renal Dose Adjustments

Renal impairment is the most common reason for drug-condition dose adjustments. Estimated GFR (eGFR) from CKD-EPI equation is the standard reference.

### Key Renally Cleared Drugs Requiring Adjustment

| Drug Class | Drugs | Action at eGFR 30-59 mL/min | Action at eGFR 15-29 mL/min | Action at eGFR <15 mL/min or HD |
|------------|-------|----------------------------|----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Antimicrobials | Amoxicillin, ceftriaxone, ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, acyclovir, valacyclovir, amphotericin B | Reduce dose 50% or extend interval | Reduce dose 50-75% or extend interval further | Significant reduction or avoid; dose after HD for some |
| Opioids | Morphine (active metabolite M6G accumulates), codeine (active metabolite morphone accumulates), gabapentin, pregabalin | Reduce dose 50% | Reduce dose 75% | Avoid morphine and codeine entirely; use oxycodone (partially hepatic) with caution; gabapentin/pregabalin need major reduction |
| Antidiabetics | Metformin (lactic acidosis risk), sitagliptin, empagliflozin, canagliflozin, insulin (reduced clearance) | Metformin: max 1000mg/day BID; SGLT2i: reduce dose | Metformin: avoid if eGFR <30; SGLT2i: avoid canagliflozin | Metformin contraindicated; insulin dose reduction needed |
| Cardiovascular | Spironolactone (hyperkalemia), enoxaparin (bleeding), dabigatran, dofetilide | Spironolactone: caution; enoxaparin: reduce dose; dabigatran: reduce | Spironolactone: avoid; enoxaparin: avoid or major reduction; dabigatran: avoid | Most renally cleared cardiovascular drugs contraindicated or require major adjustment |
| Others | Allopurinol (active metabolite oxypurinol), colchicine, baclofen, lithium | Reduce dose | Significant reduction | Major reduction; lithium contraindicated |

### Practical Approach to Renal Dosing

1. Calculate eGFR using CKD-EPI (2021 equation, no race coefficient). For drug dosing, round eGFR to the nearest standard cutoff (60, 45, 30, 15).
2. Consult a renal dosing reference (e.g., AHFS Drug Information, UpToDate Lexidrug) for each drug. Do not rely on memory for dose adjustments.
3. For drugs with narrow therapeutic indices (vancomycin, aminoglycosides, digoxin, lithium), always obtain serum drug levels.
4. Reassess renal function regularly: at least every 3 months for stable CKD, within 1 week of any acute illness in CKD patients, and before every dose of nephrotoxic drugs (aminoglycosides, amphotericin, IV contrast).

## Hepatic Cautions

Hepatic impairment affects drug metabolism unpredictably because liver function does not decline as uniformly as renal function. The Child-Pugh classification (score based on bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites, encephalopathy) is the primary tool.

### Child-Pugh Classification

| Parameter | 1 Point | 2 Points | 3 Points |
|-----------|---------|----------|----------|
| Bilirubin (mg/dL) | <2.0 | 2.0-3.0 | >3.0 |
| Albumin (g/dL) | >3.5 | 2.8-3.5 | <2.8 |
| INR | <1.7 | 1.7-2.2 | >2.2 |
| Ascites | None | Mild-moderate | Severe/refractory |
| Encephalopathy | None | Grade I-II (suppressed with meds) | Grade III-IV (refractory) |

Child-Pugh A (5-6): mild impairment. Most drugs can be used with caution.
Child-Pugh B (7-9): moderate impairment. Dose reduction of 25-50% for hepatically cleared drugs.
Child-Pugh C (10-15): severe impairment. Avoid hepatically cleared drugs when possible.

### Key Hepatically Cleared Drugs

| Drug | Child-Pugh A | Child-Pugh B | Child-Pugh C |
|------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Acetaminophen | Max 2g/day | Max 1.5g/day | Avoid or max 1g/day with monitoring |
| Benzodiazepines (lorazepam preferred — glucuronidation preserved) | Use with caution | Reduce dose 50% | Avoid (except lorazepam at lowest dose) |
| Statins (simvastatin, atorvastatin) | Use with caution | Avoid simvastatin; reduce atorvastatin 50% | Avoid all statins except low-dose pravastatin |
| NSAIDs | Use lowest dose, shortest duration | Avoid if possible | Contraindicated (hepatotoxicity, bleeding, renal hypoperfusion) |
| Opioids (oxycodone, tramadol) | Reduce dose 50% | Reduce dose 75% | Avoid |
| Beta-blockers (propranolol, carvedilol) | Use for variceal prophylaxis | Reduce dose; monitor for hypotension | Caution; may precipitate encephalopathy |
| DOACs (rivaroxaban, apixaban) | Use with caution | Avoid rivaroxaban; apixaban: reduce dose | Contraindicated |
| Fluconazole | Standard dose | Reduce 50% | Avoid or reduce 50% |

## Pregnancy Categories and Lactation

Pregnancy creates a unique drug-condition interaction where the "condition" is the physiological state of pregnancy and the presence of a developing fetus.

### FDA Pregnancy Categories (Legacy, Still Widely Referenced)

| Category | Meaning | Examples |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **A** | Adequate human studies show no risk | Few drugs: levothyroxine, prenatal vitamins, folic acid |
| **B** | Animal studies show no risk; no adequate human studies OR animal studies show risk but human studies do not | Amoxicillin, metformin, ondansetron, pantoprazole |
| **C** | Animal studies show risk; no adequate human studies. Use only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to fetus | Fluoroquinolones, most SSRIs, gabapentin, prednisone |
| **D** | Positive evidence of human fetal risk, but benefits may outweigh risks in serious situations | ACE inhibitors, ARBs (second/third trimester), phenytoin, valproic acid, NSAIDs (third trimester) |
| **X** | Contraindicated — animal or human studies show fetal abnormalities and/or the risk clearly outweighs any benefit | Isotretinoin, methotrexate, warfarin, lithium (first trimester), ACE inhibitors (all trimesters), diethylstilbestrol |

### Key Drugs to Avoid in Pregnancy

- **Isotretinoin:** 20-35% major malformation rate (CNS, cardiac, ear). iPledge program required. Must have two forms of contraception and negative pregnancy tests.
- **ACE inhibitors and ARBs:** Oligohydramnios, renal agenesis, pulmonary hypoplasia, skull ossification defects (second and third trimester). First trimester exposure may also increase cardiac and CNS malformations.
- **NSAIDs (third trimester):** Premature closure of ductus arteriosus, oligohydramnios, fetal renal impairment. Low-dose aspirin (81mg) for preeclampsia prophylaxis is safe.
- **Warfarin:** Warfarin embryopathy (nasal hypoplasia, stippled epiphyses) in first trimester; CNS abnormalities from hemorrhage throughout pregnancy. Use LMWH (enoxaparin) instead.
- **Valproic acid:** Neural tube defects (1-2%), cognitive impairment, autism spectrum. Avoid in women of childbearing age unless no alternatives exist.
- **Tetracyclines:** Discoloration and hypoplasia of teeth, bone growth inhibition. Avoid after 16 weeks gestation.

### Lactation Considerations

- Most drugs are present in breast milk to some degree. The relative infant dose (RID) — the dose the infant receives via milk as a percentage of the weight-adjusted maternal dose — is the key metric. RID less than 10% is generally considered acceptable.
- Drugs with high RID or known infant toxicity: lithium (RID ~40%), radioactive iodine (concentrates in thyroid), cytotoxic chemotherapy, ergotamine, bromocriptine.
- Consult LactMed (TOXNET database) for specific drug-lactation data.

## Monitoring Parameters

1. **Baseline organ function assessment** before starting any high-risk drug: eGFR, serum creatinine, BUN, liver panel (AST, ALT, ALP, bilirubin, albumin, INR), CBC.
2. **Reassessment timing:** Renal function every 3 months for stable CKD, within 48-72 hours after any acute kidney injury. Hepatic function at baseline and with any clinical change (jaundice, ascites, encephalopathy).
3. **Drug levels:** Obtain trough levels for narrow-therapeutic-index drugs at steady state (typically 5 half-lives after initiation or dose change). Vancomycin AUC-guided dosing is preferred over trough-only monitoring.
4. **Pregnancy testing:** For any woman of childbearing age starting a Category D or X drug, document a negative pregnancy test before dispensing.
5. **Medication reconciliation:** At every care transition, review the drug list against the current problem list specifically for drug-condition interactions. The introduction of a new diagnosis (e.g., newly diagnosed CKD) should trigger a full review of all existing medications.
