# Georgian Sauce Techniques

Georgian cuisine features a rich tradition of sauces built around three main families: (1) Tkemali — a tart plum sauce; (2) Satsivi — a cold walnut-garlic sauce served with poultry; and (3) Bazhe — a simpler warm walnut sauce. Other notable sauces include Adjika (spicy pepper paste) and garlic-matsoni (yogurt) sauces.

Core technique for walnut-based sauces (Satsivi/Bazhe): walnuts are ground to a fine paste (traditionally through a meat grinder or mortar) until they release their oil. The paste is thinned with boiled water or broth, then seasoned with garlic, spices, and acid. For Satsivi, the sauce is simmered gently with poultry; for Bazhe, it is served fresh and unheated. Tkemali is made by boiling sour plums (or cherry plums) until soft, passing through a sieve, then cooking the purée with garlic, herbs, and spices until thickened.

Essential ingredients across Georgian sauces: walnuts (the backbone of Satsivi/Bazhe), sour plums or tkemali fruit, garlic, fresh cilantro, blue fenugreek (utskho suneli), marigold petals (khmeli suneli), coriander, vinegar or pomegranate juice, and hot pepper.

Representative recipe — Satsivi (walnut sauce for chicken): Grind 500g shelled walnuts to a fine paste. Mince 6–8 garlic cloves with salt. Mix walnut paste with garlic, 1 tsp ground coriander, 1 tsp blue fenugreek, ½ tsp cinnamon, ½ tsp ground cloves, and a pinch of cayenne. Gradually stir in 1–1.5 liters of warm chicken broth until smooth. Add 2–3 tbsp white wine vinegar or pomegranate juice. Pour over cooked chicken pieces and simmer 10–15 minutes without boiling. Serve cold or at room temperature, garnished with pomegranate seeds and cilantro.
