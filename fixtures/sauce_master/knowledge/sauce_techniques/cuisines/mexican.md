# Mexican Sauce Techniques

MEXICAN SAUCE TECHNIQUES — SYNTHESIS

1. KEY SAUCE FAMILIES/TYPES
- Salsas Frescas (raw, uncooked): pico de gallo, salsa verde cruda, salsa roja cruda. Chopped or blended fresh ingredients, no cooking.
- Salsas Cocidas (cooked): salsa roja, salsa verde, salsa ranchera. Ingredients are roasted, boiled, or charred then blended.
- Moles: complex, slow-cooked sauces. Mole Poblano (chocolate, chiles, nuts), Mole Verde (pumpkin seeds, tomatillos, herbs), Mole Negro (darkest, Oaxacan), Coloradito, Amarillo, etc. Can have 20+ ingredients.
- Adobos: chile-based marinades/pastes, often with vinegar, used for meats (e.g., adobo de chipotle).
- Pipianes: pumpkin-seed-thickened sauces, related to moles but simpler.

2. CORE TECHNIQUE STEPS
- Dried chile prep: toast on dry comal/skillet 30–60 sec per side until fragrant (not burnt, or they turn bitter). Then soak in hot water 15–20 min to rehydrate.
- Roasting/charring: tomatoes, tomatillos, onions, garlic, fresh chiles are charred on a comal or under a broiler until blistered and blackened in spots — this is the foundation of smoky depth.
- Grinding/blending: traditionally in a molcajete (volcanic stone mortar) for texture; modern method uses blender. For moles, ingredients are ground in stages.
- Frying the sauce (freír la salsa): blended salsa is fried in hot oil for 5–10 min to deepen flavor, thicken, and "cook" the raw taste out.
- Mole building: spices and seeds are toasted, ground; chiles are toasted and soaked; everything is blended with broth, then simmered low and slow (1–3 hours), stirred constantly to prevent scorching.

3. ESSENTIAL INGREDIENTS
- Dried chiles: ancho, guajillo, pasilla, mulato, chipotle, árbol (heat), cascabel.
- Fresh chiles: jalapeño, serrano, poblano, habanero.
- Tomatoes (jitomate) and tomatillos (tomate verde) — the base of most salsas.
- Aromatics: white onion, garlic, cilantro, epazote.
- Mole-specific: Mexican chocolate, almonds/peanuts, sesame seeds, raisins, plantain, stale tortilla (thickener), cinnamon, cloves, cumin, allspice.
- Acid: lime juice or vinegar for brightness.
- Fat: lard or vegetable oil for frying the sauce.

4. REPRESENTATIVE RECIPE — SALSA ROJA COCIDA (Cooked Red Salsa)
Ingredients:
- 4 Roma tomatoes (or 3 medium tomatoes)
- 2–3 dried guajillo chiles (or 2 fresh serranos for heat)
- 1/4 white onion, roughly chopped
- 2 garlic cloves, unpeeled
- 1 tbsp vegetable oil or lard
- Salt to taste (~1/2 tsp)
- Optional: pinch of dried Mexican oregano

Method:
1. Toast dried guajillos on a hot dry comal 30 sec per side. Soak in hot water 15 min. Drain, discard stems and seeds.
2. On the same comal, char tomatoes, onion, and garlic until blistered and softened (8–10 min, turning). Peel garlic.
3. Blend charred vegetables, soaked chiles, and 1/4 cup water until smooth.
4. Heat oil in a saucepan over medium-high. Pour in the blended salsa (it will splatter). Fry, stirring, for 5–7 min until darkened and thickened.
5. Season with salt and oregano. Simmer 2 more min. Serve warm.

This technique — char, blend, fry — is the backbone of countless Mexican table salsas.
