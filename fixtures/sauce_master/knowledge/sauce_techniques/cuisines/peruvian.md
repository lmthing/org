# Peruvian Sauce Techniques

Peruvian sauce-making centers on two iconic families: (1) Huancaína — a creamy, mildly spicy cheese sauce served cold over boiled potatoes, and (2) Ocopa — a richer, herbaceous sauce from Arequipa thickened with huacatay (black mint) and peanuts or walnuts. Both belong to the broader category of ají-based cold sauces that define Peruvian home cooking.

Core technique: The foundational step is making a pasta de ají (pepper paste) by rehydrating dried ají amarillo or ají mirasol, then blending with oil. For huancaína, this paste is blended with queso fresco, evaporated milk, and saltine crackers until silky. For ocopa, the paste is combined with toasted peanuts, huacatay leaves, onion, garlic, and sometimes a touch of cheese, then thinned with milk or broth. Both sauces are emulsified in a blender and served cold — no cooking required after blending.

Essential ingredients: ají amarillo (yellow chili pepper) is the backbone, providing fruity heat and vibrant color. Queso fresco or feta adds tang and body. Evaporated milk creates creaminess without heaviness. Saltine crackers or bread act as thickeners. Huacatay (Peruvian black mint) is the signature herb for ocopa, irreplaceable for authenticity. Toasted peanuts or walnuts add depth.

Representative recipe — Huancaína Sauce: Soak 2 dried ají amarillo peppers (or use 3 tbsp jarred paste). Blend with 200g queso fresco, 1/2 cup evaporated milk, 4 saltine crackers, 1 clove garlic, and 2 tbsp oil. Season with salt. Thin with additional milk to desired consistency. Serve over sliced boiled potatoes with hard-boiled eggs and olives.
