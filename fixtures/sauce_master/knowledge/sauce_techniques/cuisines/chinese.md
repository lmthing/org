# Chinese Sauce Techniques

Chinese wok sauces revolve around fermented pastes and soy-based seasonings that build depth through layering. Key sauce families include: (1) Brown/Master sauces — soy sauce, oyster sauce, hoisin, and bean pastes for braising and stir-frying; (2) Clear/White sauces — light soy, Shaoxing wine, and stock for delicate seafood and vegetables; (3) Sweet-sour sauces — black vinegar, sugar, and ketchup/plum sauce; (4) Spicy fermented sauces — doubanjiang (fermented broad bean chili paste), douchi (fermented black beans), and chili oils.

Core technique steps: Heat wok until smoking, add cold oil (hot wok, cold oil prevents sticking), bloom aromatics (garlic, ginger, scallion whites) for 10-15 seconds, then add fermented pastes and fry briefly to release fragrance before liquids. Swirl sauce down the sides of the wok to caramelize on contact. Use cornstarch slurry (1:2 starch to water) at the end for velveting and gloss. Work fast — total cooking time often under 2 minutes.

Essential ingredients: Light soy sauce (saltiness), dark soy sauce (color), Shaoxing wine (aroma), oyster sauce (umami), doubanjiang (spicy fermented depth), fermented black beans (pungent saltiness), Chinkiang black vinegar (mellow acidity), sesame oil (finishing), cornstarch (thickening), and sugar or rock sugar (balance).

Representative recipe — All-Purpose Brown Stir-Fry Sauce (enough for 500g protein + vegetables): 3 tbsp light soy sauce, 1 tbsp dark soy sauce, 2 tbsp oyster sauce, 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine, 1 tsp sugar, 1/2 tsp white pepper, 1 tsp sesame oil, 1 tbsp cornstarch mixed with 3 tbsp water. Combine all in a bowl. Stir-fry protein first, remove, cook aromatics and vegetables, return protein, pour sauce around edges of wok, toss until glossy and thickened (30 seconds).
