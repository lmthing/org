# French Sauce Techniques

French mother sauces are the foundation of classical French cuisine, codified by Auguste Escoffier. The five mother sauces are: (1) Béchamel (white sauce, milk + white roux), (2) Velouté (light stock + blond roux), (3) Espagnole (brown stock + brown roux, tomato paste, mirepoix), (4) Sauce Tomat (tomato-based, often with pork, aromatics, roux), and (5) Hollandaise (emulsified egg yolks + clarified butter, acidulated with lemon or vinegar). From these, hundreds of derivative sauces are built: Mornay (Béchamel + cheese), Sauce Suprême (Velouté + cream), Sauce Bordelaise (Espagnole + red wine, shallots, bone marrow), and Béarnaise (Hollandaise + tarragon, shallot reduction).

Core technique steps: (1) Prepare the roux — equal parts flour and butter cooked to white, blond, or brown stage depending on the sauce. (2) Gradually whisk in cold liquid (milk, stock) to prevent lumps, then simmer 20–30 minutes to cook out raw flour taste. (3) For emulsified sauces (Hollandaise), whisk egg yolks with a reduction over gentle heat, then slowly drizzle warm clarified butter while whisking constantly. (4) Season and strain. Derivatives are made by adding ingredients (cheese, cream, reductions, purées) to the finished mother sauce.

Essential ingredients: butter, all-purpose flour, whole milk, veal/chicken/fish stock, tomatoes, egg yolks, clarified butter, lemon juice or white wine vinegar, mirepoix (onion, carrot, celery), salt, white pepper, nutmeg.

Representative recipe — Béchamel (yields ~2 cups): Melt 2 tbsp butter in a saucepan over medium heat. Whisk in 2 tbsp flour and cook 1–2 minutes (white roux, do not brown). Gradually whisk in 2 cups whole milk. Add 1/4 tsp salt, pinch of white pepper, pinch of nutmeg. Simmer gently, stirring frequently, 15–20 minutes until thickened and no raw flour taste remains. For Mornay: stir in 1/2 cup grated Gruyère and 2 tbsp Parmesan off heat.
