# Small business / maker — split INPUTS from OUTPUTS

A one-person production or retail business splits by real-world SUBJECT like anything else — the trap
here is treating it all as one "inventory." Two subjects almost always stay separate, however similar
their records look side by side:

- **Materials / supplies** — the inputs consumed to make or run the business (raw stock, ingredients,
  components, packaging). A genuine advice subject: what a material actually is, a cheaper or closer
  source, a substitute, and when to reorder are questions with real researchable answers.
- **Products / catalog** — the finished items sold. A different subject: pricing, presentation, and
  which line a new or one-off item belongs to.

Do not fold these into one specialist for brevity just because both look like "stock" in the same
spreadsheet or CSV — the user asks a different KIND of question of each (what should I use / where do
I source it, vs. what should I charge / how do I list it). If one side is genuinely thin, keep the
substantive one as a specialist and leave the other as a plain table; don't merge a materials subject
that has real sourcing questions into the product catalog just to save a specialist.

**Suppliers/vendors** are their own specialist when the user would ask "who else could supply this" —
terms, contacts, lead times, alternates. **Sales, orders, and customers** are DATA (tables); split out a
channel or account specialist only when there is standing knowledge beyond the transactions themselves
(a wholesale account's terms, a recurring client's preferences).
