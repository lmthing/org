# Volunteered intent — the general heuristic

A volunteered item is ambiguous enough to ASK (store-vs-remind) only when its intended future
behaviour is genuinely unsettled. Decide from the signals below, never from surface grammar alone.

## Ask when ANY of these fire

- **Unstated desired future behaviour.** The item could be either a passive fact to keep in mind OR an
  active reminder that should fire on its own at some later point — and nothing they said picks between
  them. "Keep this front of mind" phrasings are the tell: the mechanism (remember vs. remind) is named
  nowhere.
- **A required slot for the action reading is missing.** If read as a reminder it would need a WHEN
  (and sometimes a WHAT-kind) to fire, and that slot is absent. An item that names the thing but not
  when/whether it should resurface is unsettled by construction.
- **The verb signals "don't let this slip" with no home named.** The user is flagging that the item
  MATTERS and must not be lost, without saying where it should live or how it should come back.

## Do NOT ask — just STORE — when the item is a plain fact with a determinable home

This store-outright path applies ONLY when NONE of the ask-signals above fire — in particular, only
when the item carries NO "keep this front of mind" / "don't forget" / "don't let this slip" phrasing.
When such a phrasing IS present and the future behaviour is unstated, the ambiguity wins and you ASK,
even if the item also carries a storable value (see the riding-facts trap below).

- A stated value, amount, payment, name, date, or correction with a clear place to live — AND no
  keep-in-mind phrasing riding on it — is a plain declarative fact. Store it (or update the row it
  corrects). Asking here is the OTHER failure — interrogating instead of acting.
- A preference or standing instruction about the user ("call me by my first name") is a stored
  preference, not an ambiguous reminder.

## Traps — these do NOT resolve the ambiguity

- **Emphasis is not an answer.** Saying it with feeling ("I really can't let this slip") tells you it
  matters, not which of the two they want. Urgency answers "does it matter", never "remember or remind".
- **Riding facts are not an answer.** The sentence may carry one concrete fact or several in the same
  breath; a run of real details riding along with a "keep this in mind" phrase does not settle the
  mechanism question underneath it. Capture the facts, but the intent is still ambiguous. In
  particular, a riding stated AMOUNT (a price, a fee, a payment) does NOT qualify the item as "a plain
  fact with a determinable home" and does NOT license storing it outright — its determinable home is
  where the fact goes AFTER the user answers remember-vs-remind, not a reason to skip the ask. The
  keep-in-mind phrasing DOMINATES the riding value.
- **The grammatical subject does not matter.** Whether the forgotten thing is the subject or the
  rememberer is ("don't let it slip" vs. "don't let me forget"), it is the same ambiguity.

When the ambiguity signals fire and none of the store-outright signals do, name the TWO things you
could do in one plain sentence — just record it, or set a reminder — and ASK which they mean. After
they answer, do exactly that; do not invent a mechanism you do not have.
